# Trellis

**A garden wall where vines race to strangle each other.**

Six vines sprout on a hexagonal trellis. Every tick, every vine grows one cell —
straight on, or sixty degrees to the left or right. A vine that grows into the
wall's edge, into anyone's stem (its own included), or into the same cell as
another vine at the same instant, wilts and blooms where it died. The last vine
growing wins the wall.

That is the whole game. It is a light-cycle game played on hexes by plants,
and it is the oldest kind of multiplayer fun: everyone moves at once and
nobody can stop.

## Why it is delightful

The other five vines are *gardeners* — small search engines with tempers.

- **Ivy** counts territory: it floods the trellis from every head at once and
  steers to own the most cells closer to it than to anyone else.
- **Kudzu** is a strangler: it closes on whichever vine is nearest and tries to
  shrink *that* vine's territory to nothing.
- **Moss** is a survivor: it hugs walls and stems so it wastes no space.
- **Hops** is a wild card with a seeded die.

When a vine is sealed off from everyone else, every gardener stops fighting and
starts *packing*: it coils into its pocket, wall-hugging, to outlast the others.
That switch is the moment: you watch Kudzu cut Ivy's half of the wall in two,
Ivy realise it is alone, and spend its last forty ticks folding itself into a
tight green spiral while Kudzu runs out of room one cell sooner.

## The moment

You join from the Arena, take the coral vine, and steer with ← and →. Twelve
seconds in, Kudzu swings across your bow and you realise it was never chasing
Moss. You turn, the corridor closes, and your vine blooms against its stem. You
press *Join* again before the next round has sprouted.

## What else

- **The Arena** never stops. Rounds play themselves out among the gardeners
  whether or not anyone is watching, and every browser sees the same wall over
  live events. Humans who join take a seat in the next round.
- **The Herbarium** presses every finished round flat: a botanical plate of
  where every vine went and where it bloomed. Each pressing is stored as a seed
  and the humans' keystrokes — nothing else — and is regrown on demand.
- **The Potting Shed** runs the whole engine in the browser: play alone
  against the gardeners, or seat six of them and watch.

## Where it could go

Team vines (two vines that may cross each other's stems). Gardeners that learn
— a tournament that ticks overnight and breeds the scoring weights. Thorns and
seeds on the wall. A desktop build with a gamepad. Ranked pressings by the
length of the winning vine.
